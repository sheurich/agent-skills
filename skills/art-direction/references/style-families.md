# Style families

Sixteen broad design families for use in art-direction workflows. Each
profile is a compact design vocabulary, not a copy of a historical artifact.
Named movements are cited history. They describe traits an option may
borrow. They are not commands to imitate a specific poster, artist, or
brand.

A direction may combine at most two families. State what each family
contributes to the combination.

## Rational typographic

- Use for: technical, civic, or institutional subjects that need to read as
  precise and objective.
- Design logic: a visible grid carries meaning. Hierarchy comes from scale
  and position, not decoration.
- Composition: modular grid, asymmetric balance, generous margins, strong
  alignment between text blocks.
- Typography: one or two grotesque or humanist sans families. Type is the
  primary visual element.
- Color: restrained palette, often one accent against black, white, or gray.
- Imagery: sparse. Photography or diagrams appear only when they carry
  information.
- Material: flat, even surfaces. No simulated texture or wear.
- Motion: linear transitions, grid-aligned reveals, no bounce or overshoot.
- Failure modes: reads as a stock corporate template when the grid has no
  point of tension or the accent color is arbitrary.
- Do not copy mechanically: do not force a strict grid onto content with
  irregular structure just to signal seriousness.
- Source anchors: [Swiss](https://john.hartnup.uk/poster-prompts/swiss-international/), [Bauhaus](https://john.hartnup.uk/poster-prompts/bauhaus-modernist/)

## Geometric modernist

- Use for: subjects about force, change, or systems in motion, where the
  message benefits from visible structural tension.
- Design logic: basic shapes and diagonals carry meaning on their own,
  independent of naturalistic imagery.
- Composition: overlapping planes, diagonal thrust, deliberate spatial
  tension between elements.
- Typography: geometric or constructed letterforms, often integrated into
  the shape composition rather than set apart from it.
- Color: a small set of flat, high-contrast colors, frequently red, black,
  and one neutral.
- Imagery: abstracted shapes standing in for people, machines, or forces.
  Photography, when used, is cropped and reassembled.
- Material: flat color fields. No gradients or photographic texture.
- Motion: directional movement along diagonals, shapes assembling or
  colliding into position.
- Failure modes: becomes decorative wallpaper when the shapes lose their
  directional logic and just fill space.
- Do not copy mechanically: do not attach red-and-black diagonal shapes to
  unrelated subject matter as a generic "bold" signal.
- Source anchors: [Constructivist](https://john.hartnup.uk/poster-prompts/constructivist/), [De Stijl](https://john.hartnup.uk/poster-prompts/de-stijl/)

## Organic decorative

- Use for: subjects tied to craft, nature, wellbeing, or heritage, where
  warmth matters more than speed or precision.
- Design logic: structure follows natural curves. Borders, pattern, and
  lettering integrate into one continuous decorative surface.
- Composition: flowing asymmetric frames, curved borders that contain and
  guide the content.
- Typography: lettering with organic curves, often custom-drawn and woven
  into the border or illustration.
- Color: muted, natural tones with occasional gold or jewel-tone accents.
- Imagery: stylized plant forms, flowing hair or drapery, and figures
  rendered as part of the pattern rather than isolated on a background.
- Material: the surface reads as printed or painted, with visible line
  quality rather than photographic realism.
- Motion: slow, curving transitions that echo the linework.
- Failure modes: turns busy and illegible when pattern density competes with
  the text it is meant to frame.
- Do not copy mechanically: do not apply decorative floral borders to
  technical or safety-critical content where they compete with legibility.
- Source anchors: [Art Nouveau](https://john.hartnup.uk/poster-prompts/art-nouveau/), [Arts and Crafts](https://john.hartnup.uk/poster-prompts/arts-and-crafts/)

## Streamlined geometric

- Use for: subjects about aspiration, luxury, or engineered progress that
  benefit from a controlled, symmetrical sense of scale.
- Design logic: stepped, symmetrical geometry and metallic contrast signal
  controlled, deliberate luxury rather than raw force.
- Composition: centered or mirrored symmetry, stepped or sunburst forms
  radiating from a focal point.
- Typography: geometric display letterforms with even stroke widths and
  wide, confident spacing.
- Color: metallic or jewel tones against black or deep neutrals, sharp
  contrast rather than blend.
- Imagery: stylized machines, skylines, or figures rendered with smooth,
  simplified curves.
- Material: crisp edges suggesting lacquer, chrome, or polished stone. No
  visible grain or wear.
- Motion: measured, symmetrical movement, elements sliding into alignment.
- Failure modes: reads as a costume-party pastiche when gold-and-black
  styling is applied without the underlying symmetry and stepped structure.
- Do not copy mechanically: do not add metallic gradients to an unrelated
  layout and call it this family.
- Source anchors: [Art Deco](https://john.hartnup.uk/poster-prompts/art-deco/), [Streamline Moderne](https://john.hartnup.uk/poster-prompts/streamline-moderne/)

## Editorial

- Use for: subjects with a reading sequence: reports, articles, briefings,
  and other content meant to be read start to finish.
- Design logic: a clear reading hierarchy paces the content. Display and
  text type play distinct, consistent roles.
- Composition: modular column grid, disciplined whitespace, consistent
  spacing rhythm between sections.
- Typography: a serif or text-optimized sans for body copy, paired with a
  distinct display face for headings and pull quotes.
- Color: mostly neutral, with one or two accent colors reserved for
  navigation cues, callouts, or data.
- Imagery: photography or charts placed to support the argument, captioned
  and credited, not decorative.
- Material: flat, print-like surface. Paper texture is optional and subtle.
- Motion: minimal; if present, supports scroll-based reading order.
- Failure modes: becomes a generic template when every section uses the
  same weight and size with no deliberate pacing.
- Do not copy mechanically: do not import a magazine's specific typeface
  pairing without checking it supports the actual content's data and length.
- Source anchors: [Contemporary Editorial](https://john.hartnup.uk/poster-prompts/contemporary-editorial/)

## Expressive typographic

- Use for: subjects with an attitude to project: music, culture, or dissent,
  where tone matters as much as information.
- Design logic: type itself changes scale, angle, spacing, and overlap to
  carry emotional tone, not just legibility.
- Composition: layered, overlapping type blocks at varied angles, tension
  between order and disruption.
- Typography: mixed weights and styles in one composition, extreme scale
  contrast, letters that touch, overlap, or run off the frame.
- Color: high contrast, sometimes clashing, chosen for energy over harmony.
- Imagery: secondary to type, when present at all. Photocopied or
  distressed textures may accompany the type.
- Material: visible reproduction artifacts: photocopy grain, halftone
  breakup, or torn edges, used deliberately.
- Motion: abrupt, energetic transitions, type that shakes or snaps into
  place.
- Failure modes: becomes noise when every element competes for attention
  with no single dominant hierarchy.
- Do not copy mechanically: do not scatter distressed textures onto content
  that needs calm, unambiguous reading.
- Source anchors: [New Wave Typography](https://john.hartnup.uk/poster-prompts/new-wave-typography/), [Grunge Typography](https://john.hartnup.uk/poster-prompts/grunge-typography/)

## Collage and assemblage

- Use for: subjects about contradiction, critique, or multiple voices, where
  juxtaposition itself is the argument.
- Design logic: meaning comes from putting mismatched fragments next to each
  other, not from a single unified image.
- Composition: cut fragments at conflicting scales, overlapping layers,
  visible edges between sources.
- Typography: mixed found and set type, often stamped, typed, or cut from
  other sources rather than uniformly designed.
- Color: whatever the source fragments carry, often unified only by a
  limited background palette.
- Imagery: photocopied, cut, or scanned fragments of photographs, text, and
  found objects.
- Material: visible paper edges, tape, staples, or scan artifacts as part of
  the final surface.
- Motion: abrupt cuts between fragments rather than smooth transitions.
- Failure modes: reads as random clutter when fragments have no argument
  connecting their juxtaposition.
- Do not copy mechanically: do not scatter random clip art and call the
  result collage; the cuts must make a point.
- Source anchors: [Dada Collage](https://john.hartnup.uk/poster-prompts/dada-collage/), [Zine Cut-and-Paste](https://john.hartnup.uk/poster-prompts/zine-cut-and-paste/)

## Limited-print

- Use for: subjects that benefit from a handmade, small-run feel: community
  events, independent releases, or grassroots campaigns.
- Design logic: a small fixed set of spot colors and visible print
  limitations shape the composition, not a full-color simulation.
- Composition: shapes and type sized to work with flat spot-color layers,
  deliberate overprint where two colors mix.
- Typography: bold, simple letterforms that hold up as a single flat color
  layer.
- Color: two to four spot colors with intentional overprint mixing, chosen
  from a limited printable set.
- Imagery: flat, halftone-free illustration built from the same color
  layers as the type.
- Material: visible grain, slight misregistration between layers, and paper
  show-through.
- Motion: usually static; if animated, colors shift as separate flat layers.
- Failure modes: loses its identity when rendered with smooth gradients or
  photographic color depth the process could not actually produce.
- Do not copy mechanically: do not simulate misregistration on content that
  will be produced digitally with no physical print constraint.
- Source anchors: [Risograph](https://john.hartnup.uk/poster-prompts/risograph/), [Screenprint Pop](https://john.hartnup.uk/poster-prompts/screenprint-pop/)

## Symbolic illustrative

- Use for: subjects with an abstract or difficult idea that one strong
  metaphor can carry better than literal description.
- Design logic: one illustrated image, built as metaphor, carries the
  concept. Type stays subordinate to that image.
- Composition: a single dominant illustrated form, generous surrounding
  space, minimal competing elements.
- Typography: small, restrained, placed to not compete with the central
  image.
- Color: a limited, often unexpected palette chosen to support the
  metaphor's mood.
- Imagery: one clear metaphorical illustration rather than a literal scene
  or a photograph.
- Material: flat illustrated surface, minimal texture, focus on the shape
  of the metaphor.
- Motion: minimal; if present, reveals or reframes the central metaphor.
- Failure modes: becomes confusing when the metaphor requires unexplained
  cultural or personal context the audience does not share.
- Do not copy mechanically: do not select a striking image with no
  connection to the actual subject.
- Source anchors: [Polish Poster School](https://john.hartnup.uk/poster-prompts/polish-poster-school/), [Surrealist](https://john.hartnup.uk/poster-prompts/surrealist/)

## Archival vernacular

- Use for: subjects with a historical, civic, or documentary character,
  where density of information signals authority.
- Design logic: dense period-accurate information structures and visible
  reproduction limits establish authenticity.
- Composition: dense stacked text blocks, multiple type sizes on one page,
  rules and borders separating sections.
- Typography: period-appropriate serif or wood-type display faces mixed
  with plain text type, varied line lengths.
- Color: limited to what the period process could produce: sepia, black,
  cream, or duotone.
- Imagery: engravings, halftone photographs, or line illustrations
  consistent with the cited period's reproduction technology.
- Material: paper aging, foxing, halftone dot pattern, or newsprint grain.
- Motion: usually static; digital adaptations may reveal content the way a
  page would be scanned.
- Failure modes: reads as costume kitsch when period cues are applied
  without matching the informational density they originally supported.
- Do not copy mechanically: do not paste sepia and grain onto a modern
  layout without matching the period's actual information structure.
- Source anchors: [Victorian Broadside](https://john.hartnup.uk/poster-prompts/victorian-broadside/), [Halftone Newsprint](https://john.hartnup.uk/poster-prompts/halftone-newsprint/)

## Handmade

- Use for: subjects about children, community, or informal craft, where
  polish would undercut the sincerity of the message.
- Design logic: irregular, evidently hand-made marks are balanced by a
  deliberate, legible hierarchy underneath them.
- Composition: loose, hand-placed elements with a clear focal point despite
  the irregularity.
- Typography: hand-lettered or crayon-style type for display, kept legible
  through size and placement rather than precision.
- Color: a small set of bright, unmixed colors applied unevenly.
- Imagery: simple, hand-drawn shapes and figures, visible brush or crayon
  strokes.
- Material: visible paper texture, crayon or paint buildup, uneven coverage.
- Motion: irregular, hand-drawn-feeling movement rather than mechanical
  easing.
- Failure modes: reads as mocking or twee when the "childlike" marks are
  applied to serious content without genuine sincerity in the hierarchy.
- Do not copy mechanically: do not apply crayon texture to technical or
  safety content where informality undermines trust.
- Source anchors: [Child Poster Paint](https://john.hartnup.uk/poster-prompts/child-paint-pro-typography/), [Crayon Folk Handmade](https://john.hartnup.uk/poster-prompts/crayon-folk-handmade/)

## Pop and psychedelic

- Use for: subjects about culture, entertainment, or celebration that
  benefit from high visual energy and repetition.
- Design logic: repetition, optical contrast, and saturated color carry
  energy. Culturally specific display type reinforces the era or scene.
- Composition: repeated motifs, radiating or tiled patterns, bold central
  focal shapes.
- Typography: rounded, bulging, or hand-drawn display letterforms, often
  distorted to fit an optical rhythm.
- Color: highly saturated, high-contrast complementary colors.
- Imagery: bold flat illustration or halftone photography, repeated or
  duplicated for optical effect.
- Material: flat color with sharp edges, or optically vibrating patterns.
- Motion: pulsing, looping, or radiating movement.
- Failure modes: becomes visually exhausting or dated kitsch when the
  saturation and repetition have no restraint or focal hierarchy.
- Do not copy mechanically: do not apply saturated psychedelic patterning as
  a default "fun" signal on unrelated professional content; use it only as
  a restrained secondary accent when the primary family needs energy.
- Source anchors: [Pop Art](https://john.hartnup.uk/poster-prompts/pop-art/), [Psychedelic Sixties](https://john.hartnup.uk/poster-prompts/psychedelic-sixties/)

## Brutalist utilitarian

- Use for: subjects that want to signal directness, resistance to polish, or
  raw functional honesty.
- Design logic: exposed structure and forceful type create deliberate
  friction instead of smoothing it away.
- Composition: raw grid lines left visible, hard edges, minimal padding
  between elements.
- Typography: default or system-feeling monospace or grotesque type, often
  set at an aggressive scale.
- Color: stark, usually black, white, and one warning-like accent color.
- Imagery: screenshots, raw data, or unretouched photography rather than
  polished illustration.
- Material: flat, unpolished surfaces. No gradients, shadows, or rounded
  corners used for softening.
- Motion: abrupt, mechanical, no easing curves.
- Failure modes: reads as broken or unfinished when the roughness has no
  underlying intentional hierarchy holding it together.
- Do not copy mechanically: do not remove polish from content that needs
  approachability just to signal edginess.
- Source anchors: [Brutalist Graphic Design](https://john.hartnup.uk/poster-prompts/brutalist-graphic/)

## Digital systemic

- Use for: subjects about software, networks, or synthetic technology that
  benefit from a screen-native, systemic feel.
- Design logic: modular systems and synthetic surfaces read as native to
  screens rather than translated from print.
- Composition: modular panels, layered depth, screen-native spacing units.
- Typography: geometric sans or monospace type, often with synthetic
  chrome, glow, or gradient treatments.
- Color: synthetic gradients, chrome, or neon against dark or metallic
  backgrounds.
- Imagery: 3D-rendered objects, UI fragments, or generated textures rather
  than photography.
- Material: glossy, reflective, or glitched surfaces, with controlled
  digital artifacts as an intentional trait.
- Motion: fluid, physics-based, or deliberately glitching transitions.
- Failure modes: collapses into a generic "dark developer dashboard" when
  the chrome and glow have no connection to the subject's actual system.
- Do not copy mechanically: do not default to a dark gradient background
  with neon accents as a stand-in for "technical" without a subject-specific
  reason.
- Source anchors: [Y2K Chrome](https://john.hartnup.uk/poster-prompts/y2k-chrome/), [Glitch Art](https://john.hartnup.uk/poster-prompts/glitch-art/)

## Photographic cinematic

- Use for: subjects with a real place, person, or moment that benefits from
  narrative framing rather than abstraction.
- Design logic: cropping, focal depth, and sequencing carry the narrative.
  The image leads; type supports it.
- Composition: off-center framing, depth through foreground and background
  separation, negative space reserved for title treatment.
- Typography: understated display type placed to not compete with the
  photograph's focal point.
- Color: graded, mood-driven color rather than flat design color.
- Imagery: photography or photographic composites with real depth and
  lighting, not flat illustration.
- Material: film grain, lens characteristics, or color-grade treatments
  appropriate to the cited period or region.
- Motion: slow pans, focus pulls, or parallax that echo cinematic camera
  movement.
- Failure modes: reads as generic stock photography when the framing and
  grade have no connection to the subject's real setting.
- Do not copy mechanically: do not apply a specific regional cinema's color
  grade to unrelated subject matter as decoration.
- Source anchors: [East German DEFA](https://john.hartnup.uk/poster-prompts/east-german-defa/), [Hong Kong Cinema](https://john.hartnup.uk/poster-prompts/hong-kong-cinema/)

## Diagrammatic and wayfinding

- Use for: subjects that must communicate structure, process, or navigation
  accurately, where correctness matters more than mood.
- Design logic: a consistent visual syntax for shapes, lines, labels, and
  routes carries meaning. Every visual choice is a semantic choice.
- Composition: clear grouping, consistent spacing between related elements,
  a legend that defines every symbol used.
- Typography: plain, highly legible type at a consistent set of sizes tied
  to information level, not decoration.
- Color: a small palette where color reinforces a category, never the only
  signal for a distinction.
- Imagery: schematic shapes, icons, and lines rather than illustration or
  photography.
- Material: flat, technical-drawing surface. No texture that could be
  mistaken for a data signal.
- Motion: stepwise reveals that follow the process or route being
  explained.
- Failure modes: becomes misleading when color, shape, or line style is
  reused for two different meanings, or when the legend is missing.
- Do not copy mechanically: do not adopt blueprint or signage aesthetics
  without also adopting their discipline of one meaning per visual trait.
- Source anchors: [Wayfinding and Signage](https://john.hartnup.uk/poster-prompts/wayfinding-signage/), [Cyanotype and Blueprint](https://john.hartnup.uk/poster-prompts/cyanotype/)
